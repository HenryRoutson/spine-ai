Methodology & caveats

  • What replaced the imputed point. The previous version placed a single dot at ARR × median peer multiple whenever one axis was missing, which invents precision that does not exist. Here a missing or unreliable figure becomes a band along the axis it is uncertain on, and a company uncertain on both axes becomes a box. The regression uses each band's geometric centre and weights it by 1 / (1 + log₁₀(width_x) + log₁₀(width_y)), so a wide box pulls the fit far less than a clean point.
  • Three reasons a figure becomes a band. reported — two or more good sources disagree by more than 15%, or the company disclosed a floor and guided to a higher target: Replit's $150M disclosed against its $1B year-end goal, Framer's $50M against its $100M target, Vercel's $9.3B primary mark against roughly $8.1B implied by secondary blocks on offer. stale — a single solid figure old enough that the present value is a range, with the upper bound set by the cohort's median growth over the elapsed time; Bolt's $40M is from March 2025, seventeen months ago. modeled — nothing disclosed on that axis at all, so the band spans the peer-multiple interquartile range instead of sitting at its median.
  • Peer multiple. Computed across the 8 plotted tools that have a value on both axes (point figures, or band centres), giving p25 = 17.0×, median = 21.0×, p75 = 25.8×. Base44 and v0 have no standalone valuation and get a modeled band from that IQR.
  • Products inside bigger companies. v0 and Base44 are product lines, not companies. Base44's roughly $100M ARR comes from Wix management on an earnings call, which is a primary source, but Wix assigns it no separate valuation — hence a vertical band. v0 is worse: Vercel discloses neither v0 revenue nor a v0 valuation, so its ARR band is derived from Vercel's $340M total against v0's reported 4M users on a $20/month tier with a majority of v0 revenue coming from Teams and Enterprise. That is the least reliable mark on the chart and it is drawn as a box for exactly that reason. Figma Make is the same problem with no usable anchor at all, so it is omitted from the plot.
  • ARR here is self-reported and unaudited. Several of these are run-rate figures computed by annualising one strong month, which flatters fast-growing companies and is not comparable to a public company's revenue. Cursor and Lovable in particular publish run-rate, not revenue.
  • Cursor is a category outlier, deliberately included. It is an AI code editor rather than front-end tooling (similarity 5/10) and sits two orders of magnitude up the x-axis; it is on the chart as the ceiling reference for how this cohort gets priced. Its $60B mark is an all-stock acquisition price announced 16 June 2026 and not yet closed, so it is a deal price rather than a financing mark — a different instrument from every other figure here.
  • The fit is close to a straight multiple. The regression returns an exponent of 1.00, meaning valuation scales roughly linearly with ARR across the range and the fitted line is effectively a constant ~20× multiple. R² = 0.96 sounds strong but is inflated by the log–log scale and by Cursor anchoring the top right; with 10 points, 7 of them involving a band, treat this as a visual trend and not a pricing model.
  • Scraping. Figures come from the fixed URL list in sources.json. Money tokens are attributed to the nearest metric keyword rather than by pattern match, which is what stops a Series C size being read as a valuation and a prior valuation being read as ARR. Run python landscape_scraper.py --selftest to check the extractors against fixtures covering both failure modes.
