Company Profile: MediFlow AI
COMPLIANCE ASSESSMENT, Digital Health / Clinical Decision Support, Germany
MediFlow AI (Series A, headquartered in Berlin, Germany) builds an AI-powered symptom triage and care-
coordination platform used by patients and clinics across the European Union and, more recently, the United
States. The following is a summary of the company’s current data and operational practices, prepared for
compliance assessment.
1 PRODUCT AND DATA COLLECTED
MediFlow’s mobile app collects each user’s name, email, date of birth, self-reported symptoms,
medication history, and continuous heart-rate data from connected wearables. At sign-up, users
tick a single checkbox agreeing to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, which covers analytics,
marketing, product improvement, and health processing in one combined statement.
Beyond what the triage feature needs today, the app also collects users’ GPS location and phone
contact lists “to support future social and location-based features that are still on the roadmap.”
This data is currently unused.
2 USERS AND SCALE
The platform has roughly 50,000 active users, the majority of whom are residents of the European
Economic Area. Health data is stored indefinitely so that the AI models can be retrained on the
full historical dataset; no automated deletion or retention cut-off is currently configured.
3 INFRASTRUCTURE AND SECURITY
All application traffic is served over HTTPS using TLS 1.3. The primary production database is
hosted in AWS us-east-1 (Northern Virginia) for cost and latency reasons, and European user
records are not segregated from this US deployment. Data stored in the database is not currently
encrypted at rest; the team has flagged this as a backlog item.
Engineers access systems through role-based permissions. Multi-factor authentication is en-
forced for administrator accounts but is optional for regular engineers. The company’s most recent
third-party penetration test was completed eighteen months ago; a follow-up has not yet been
scheduled.
4 AI AND AUTOMATED DECISIONS
The triage engine uses a large language model to suggest a likely cause and an urgency level for
each user’s symptoms. When the model flags a case as urgent, it is queued for a human clinician
to review before any care recommendation is sent to the user; routine cases receive an automated
explanation of the reasoning. The company keeps a registry of the three models running in pro-
duction but has not yet performed a documented bias assessment on any of them.
5 VENDORS
MediFlow relies on three sub-processors: an email provider, a cloud analytics tool, and an LLM
inference provider. Signed data processing agreements are in place with all three. The company
does not currently publish a public list of these sub-processors.
